What this tells us

$18.95 is accepted, but it's near the top. The typical customer starts to feel it's expensive at about $19.

Don't raise the naengmyeon price right now. There's also no need to cut it.

A lunch special around $14 came up several times. That could bring in price-watchers and locals at lunch. We need your food cost before we can say if it pays.

What would turn a "no" into a "yes"

  1. Good Google reviews with real photos
  2. A feature on a Japanese blog or Instagram
  3. Clear parking info and a short wait
  4. A family set for 4 at one price, around $80 to $100
  5. A lunch special around $14

The takeaway

The first three fixes cost almost nothing. They're about showing people what Yu Chun is, not changing what it is. Do those first. Set prices like the family set and lunch special only after we know food costs.

Positioning

What Yu Chun should be known for

"Positioning" is the one idea you want people to remember. Every sign, post and pitch should repeat it. We looked at three options.

Recommended Option A · The original kudzu naengmyeon shop near Ala Moana

"For Japanese visitors who plan every meal from guides and Instagram, Yu Chun is the original Honolulu kudzu naengmyeon shop near Ala Moana, not just another Korean BBQ spot."

Why

Japanese visitors were the best group (50% yes). Guidebook planners only said no because Yu Chun wasn't on their list. Japanese sites already call it the 本店 (main shop).

Watch out

"Original" and "本店" must be true. Please confirm before we use them in print.

Run alongside

Option B · The naengmyeon specialist

"For Honolulu naengmyeon lovers, Yu Chun is where black arrowroot noodles are the main event, not a side dish."

Why: naengmyeon regulars said yes 67% of the time. Best used on Google, where locals search "naengmyeon near me."

Watch out: regulars compare with their usual spot, so photos and reviews have to show the noodles are worth the switch.

Later

Option C · The easy family dinner

"For local families who share, Yu Chun is a Korean dinner with one clear price for the table."

Why: families and price-watchers asked for a set for 4 at $80 to $100.

Watch out: only works once that set exists and is priced. Hold until food costs are known.

Where to spend time

Three channels to start

We picked the places where the best customers already look before they choose a restaurant. Each one answers a specific "no" from the test.

1

Google Maps profile and reviews

Why: the #1 reason to say no was "I can't tell if it's good." Locals and visitors both check Google Maps.

Do: correct hours and phone, 20+ real photos, parking and wait info, reply to every review, table cards asking for a review.

Cost: free (cards about $30)

It's working if: 20+ new reviews in 30 days, and calls and direction requests go up.

2

Japanese Hawaii media and Instagram

Why: Japanese visitors are the best group, and they plan from these before they fly. New Year is peak travel.

Do: pitch 5 Japanese Hawaii guide sites and food accounts (for example Aloha Street, LaniLani, allhawaii). Invite creators for a meal.

Cost: pitches are free; about $70 a creator meal; paid features vary

It's working if: 2+ features go live and Japanese guests say "I saw it online."

3

Window and table signs

Why: shoppers near Ala Moana are already close. One test customer said they'd stop in if they saw it on the way.

Do: one bilingual window sign, table cards in English, Japanese and Korean.

Cost: about $130

It's working if: more lunch walk-ins week over week.

Ground rules we follow

  • No fake or paid-for reviews. Google and Yelp both ban rewarding reviews, and Yelp bans asking for them, so we only ask for Google reviews.
  • No claims we can't back up ("best in Hawaii", "famous").
  • Paid creator posts are marked as ads (#PR / #提供).
  • The test customer quotes are research only. They never appear in ads.

After this

With your costs, we can price the family set and lunch special, and work out how much you can afford to spend per new customer.
