# Marketing: Yu Chun Korean Restaurant

Built from the 20-buyer simulated panel (`founder/panel/results.md`). Simulated buyers point at what to test. They are not proof.

## What the panel says marketing must fix

| Problem | Panel count | What answers it |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Trust: "nothing tells me what other people think" | 5 of 14 passes | Google/Yelp reviews and real photos, Japanese blog/Instagram coverage |
| Price: kalbi $38.95, group totals, 20% for 5+ | 5 of 14 | A family set with a known total, a lunch special (offer work, not ads) |
| Convenience: parking, wait | 2 of 14 | Say clearly where to park and how long the wait is |

Best audiences: **Japanese tourists (50% buy)** and **naengmyeon regulars (67%)**. Guidebook followers bought 0%. That's not because they dislike it. Yu Chun just isn't on their lists. That's the biggest opening.

## Positioning

1. **(Recommended)** For Japanese visitors to Honolulu who plan every meal from guides and Instagram, Yu Chun is the original Honolulu kudzu naengmyeon shop near Ala Moana, unlike the generic Korean BBQ spots in Waikiki.
   *Evidence:* top-buying segment; guidebook followers' only blocker is "not on my list" (P002, P007, P018).
2. For Honolulu naengmyeon regulars, Yu Chun is the place where black arrowroot naengmyeon is the main dish, not a side, unlike Korean restaurants that also do noodles.
   *Evidence:* regulars bought 67%, and they want proof it beats their usual spot (P008, P010).
3. For local families who share, Yu Chun is a Korean dinner with a known total for the table, unlike ordering plate by plate.
   *Evidence:* group diners and value-conscious buyers asked for a set for 4 at $80 to $100 (P009, P016). Only works once the offer exists.

Start with 1, run 2 alongside on Google. Hold 3 until the family set is priced.

## Channels

| Channel | Why it fits | Rough cost | Worked if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Google Business Profile + reviews** | The #1 objection is "no reviews or photos." Everyone checks Google Maps, and regulars and locals search "naengmyeon near me." | Free. A table card asking for reviews costs ~$30 to print. | +20 new reviews in 30 days, more direction requests and calls (Google Insights) |
| **Japanese Hawaii media + Instagram** | The best-buying segment plans from these before the trip. Pitch Japanese Hawaii guide sites and Instagram creators (e.g. Aloha Street, LaniLani, allhawaii, Hawaii food accounts). | Pitches are free. Paid features vary, so ask for the rate card. A creator meal costs about $50 to $80 in food. | 2+ features live, and Japanese guests say "I saw it on…" (ask at the register) |
| **Window/table signs in Japanese and English** | Catches Ala Moana shoppers walking by (P015's "if I were already at Ala Moana") | ~$100 | Walk-ins at lunch up week over week |

**Not doing yet:**
- Paid social ads: no proof (reviews, coverage) to send people to yet.
- TikTok: the best buyers plan on Instagram and blogs.
- Discount apps: they train deal hunters and cut margin before costs are known.
- Mainland-tourist targeting: 0% in the panel, and they want what Japanese visitors want anyway (reviews, a guide pick).

**Timing:** Japanese travel to Hawaii peaks over New Year. Coverage published by mid-December catches people planning those trips.

## Most you can pay to win a customer

Max cost per customer = (profit per visit after food and direct costs) × 1.2 visits in the first month (panel average). Over a year, use your real repeat rate.

The profit per visit needs costs. Run `/founder-cfo` with food cost, rent and labour to fill this in.

## Three numbers to watch weekly

| Number | Where | Change something if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Google reviews | Google profile | Fewer than 4 a week by week 2. Staff aren't asking, or the card isn't on tables |
| Direction requests + calls | Google Insights | Not up from the week before launch by week 3. Photos/posts aren't landing |
| "How did you hear about us?" from Japanese guests | Ask at the register, tally on paper | Zero mentions of a blog/Instagram by Dec 21. Pitch more sites or pay for one feature |

## Rules followed

- No fake reviews, no paying or discounting for reviews (Google and Yelp ban review incentives; Yelp bans asking for reviews at all, so ask for Google only).
- Panel quotes are research. They never go in ads.
- Paid creator posts are disclosed.
